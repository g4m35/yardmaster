# Production-readiness quality gates

This hillclimb targets the existing open-source, model-independent personal hub and supported connected clients. It does not silently turn Yardmaster into a multi-tenant SaaS or modify installed native routers. Existing functionality, manual effort control, retry escalation, worker-local configuration and unmerged/user-owned work must be preserved.

Exactly five criteria apply throughout this effort. Scores use a consistent 1–10 scale: 1–3 absent or unsafe; 4–6 useful with material defects; 7–8 implemented with incomplete failure/host evidence; 9 broadly demonstrated with minor limitations; 9.5–10 requires the concrete evidence below and no unresolved material issue in that category. Missing essential evidence is unverified, never a passing score. Scores are audit judgments, not reliability percentages. Independent reviewers must cite observations and may not grant a target score from confidence or agreement.

| Criterion | Scope | Evidence required for 9.5 |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ownership and coordination | Claims, leases, dependencies, reservations, stale writes and interactive handoffs | Unique non-recycled generations fence every worker/session mutation, renewal, run and upload. Same-machine and A→B→A recovery, stale/reordered requests, cancel/reclaim and dependency/overlap scenarios pass. Current owners succeed; stale owners cannot affect their state or files through orchestration. |
| 2. Work preservation and artifact acceptance | Worktrees, cleanup, checks, review verdict gates, integration and landing | Attached/detached new commits, dirt, locks and retried work survive stale cleanup plans on hub and workers. Accepted checks/reviews bind to the exact immutable artifact. Mutating checks, moved HEAD, skipped/failed required review, concurrent integration cancellation, rollback and local fast-forward landing have regression proof. |
| 3. Process lifetime and resource accounting | Setup/coding/check/review/plan children, cancellation, restart, concurrency, usage and budgets | Every owned phase is registered before work, shares abort and capacity controls, and is reaped on normal/error/abort/restart. Fault-injected post-spawn failures and TERM-resistant descendants cannot overlap replacement work. All outcomes preserve usage exactly once with actual/estimated/unknown provenance. Concurrent budget reservations and stated overshoot bounds are tested; no unsupported hard-cap claim. |
| 4. Native integration and private fleet | Native settings/effort/cache contracts, agent/session registry, pairing, transport and reconnect | Verified supported host adapters preserve manual/native settings and enforce configured floors, expose effective launch identity and measured or explicitly unknown cache telemetry. Registry distinguishes managed/observed/stale/unconnected. Scoped revocable worker pairing, private transport requirements and durable transfer/reconnect tests demonstrate access and ownership boundaries. No universal all-agent or cache guarantee without host evidence. |
| 5. Product usability, performance and delivery | Guided setup/doctor, dashboard/control UX, documentation, install, bounded architecture and runtime cost | Isolated first-run through two connected clients/machines, discover/block/stop/inspect flows are demonstrated. Desktop/mobile rendering, keyboard operation and accessible labels are verified. Representative queue/event/API latency and resource measurements are recorded with reproducible conditions and no material regression. Typecheck/build/test, migration compatibility/recovery, release artifacts and applicable deployment smoke checks pass. |

The latest baseline evidence is in `outputs/audit-2026-11-07/`. Fresh independent Opus/GPT reviewers score the five criteria and identify actionable findings after each substantive batch. Once the average of all five supported numeric scores reaches 8, research primary documentation and comparable implementations; record relevance and limitations. All five must individually reach 9.5 before successful hillclimb completion. Release uses the existing full-send gates; no CI/security/review bypasses.
